Onus is a language for code that a model writes and a person has to trust.

pub fn recent_orders( db: sql.Db[ReadOnly, schema: "orders"], who: auth.AuthedCustomer ) -> Result[List[Order], sql.Error] may sql.read, alloc ensures forall o: Order in result: o.customer == who.id
Read-only, one schema, a caller who has already passed auth, no other effects, and every returned order belongs to that customer. The compiler proves the last line from the query. A reviewer reads this and moves on.

◆ The compiler is the only checker

There is no linter, because there is nothing left for one to say. A convention that matters becomes a claim and is checked like everything else.

  • No warnings. A diagnostic is an error.
  • Pure by default; effects in the signature.
  • Authority is handed down, never acquired.

◆ Proved, checked, or assumed — and you're told which

Every obligation ends in one of three states. The ledger says where each guarantee bottoms out, so review means reading claims, not diffs.

  • Contracts discharged by the compiler where it can.
  • Runtime checks where it can't, visibly.
  • Assumptions with a justification and a date.

◆ Trust changes over a project's life

A prototype and a hardened core live in one codebase under different rules. Nothing at a higher zone ever rests on a claim from a lower one.

  • draft — sketch freely, nothing authoritative.
  • hardened — interfaces are yours, bodies are the model's.
  • critical — every entry on a checked path.
